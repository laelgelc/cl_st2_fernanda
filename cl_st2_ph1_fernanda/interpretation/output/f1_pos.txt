## Factor f1 (positive): Comparative reporting of ENADE rating distributions

The positive pole captures a reporting practice that **organises course-level ENADE ratings into comparable distributions and guides readers through the tables and graphs used to interpret them**. The national overview establishes a common frame of reference; regional and institutional breakdowns show how the distribution varies within it.

**Corpus-wide loadings.** Several of the strongest loadings concern the representation and reading of distributions: *poligonal* (1.35), *ordenado* (1.32), *tabular* (1.31), *pontilhado* (1.29), *abscissa* (1.24) and *cumulativo* (1.24). Together with colour terms such as *roxa*, *rosa* and *laranja*, they point to prose that explains how to read plotted series, not merely to the presence of figures. A complementary cluster marks the work of constructing the comparison: *cruzar*, *entrecruzar* and *tipologia* suggest cross-classification; *ofertar*, *ministrar* and *pública* situate it in the institutional organisation of higher education; and *contar*, *equivaler* and *bimodal* support the description of counts, proportions and distributional patterns. Terms such as *panorama*, *visualizar*, *finalidade* and the strongly loading *reiterativo* also signal an explicit, recurring presentation procedure.

**What the high-scoring texts show.** All ten examples are from `2023_c5` and score 22–23. Across different fields, they follow a common sequence: announce a national overview, explain the 1–5 scale and the conditions for *sem conceito* (SC), then translate tabulated counts into percentages and compare the most frequent ratings nationally and by region. The introductions also announce cross-tabulations by administrative category, mode of provision and academic organisation. Several passages go further by telling readers how to interpret the axes and the left–right positions of cumulative curves. The full-document loading-word lists reinforce this graphical component: even when the displayed passage ends with regional counts, the document registers terms such as *poligonal*, *pontilhado*, *abscissa*, *cumulativo* and *roxa*. The repeated wording across fields illustrates a shared 2023 reporting template, not ten independent demonstrations of how every edition expressed the dimension.

**Groups driving the pole.** The project’s `c5` section (distribution of concepts) has the highest mean in every observed year. The strongest groups are `2023_c5` (21.33), `2022_c5` (18.19), `2021_c5` (17.38) and `2019_c5` (16.79). The longer trajectory is upward but not strictly monotonic: `c5` rises from 0.67 in 2009 to roughly 8–9 in 2013–2016, then steps up to about 14.3 in 2017–2018 before reaching its later peaks. The pole is therefore associated with `c5` throughout the corpus but is most pronounced in recent editions. This is a shift in the strength of the reporting discourse, not evidence that ENADE ratings themselves improved.

**Possible labels**
- **Comparative reporting of ENADE rating distributions** — preferred because it captures both the object of reporting and the function of making national, regional and institutional distributions comparable.
- **Guided comparison of course ratings** — foregrounds how the prose teaches readers to interpret tables, percentages and cumulative plots.
- **Standardised mapping of course ratings** — foregrounds the recurring categories and presentation sequence, especially visible in the 2023 examples.
