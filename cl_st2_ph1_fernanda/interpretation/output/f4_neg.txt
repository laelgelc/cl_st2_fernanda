## f4 (negative): Reporting what the exam assesses

The negative pole is best read as an **edition-sensitive discourse of documenting assessment**. Its clearest manifestation is the 2023 technical analysis, where reports describe how exam items are analysed and how student responses are evaluated. The corpus-wide loadings also point to a strand of student participation and exam administration, which may help account for the strong showing of the 2009 exam-perception section. The evidence does not establish that both editions express the pole in the same way.

**Loadings and examples.** The strongest recurring lexical cluster evokes a practical problem and possible responses: *pluvial* (−.81), *insalubre* (−.77), *deslizamento* (−.76), and *beira* (−.74) evoke hazardous conditions, while *alocar* (−.86), *factível* (−.77), and *atenuar* (−.76) suggest assessing feasible interventions. All ten 2023_c6 example documents contain these seven loading words, as well as *dedicação* and *ignorância*, despite covering fields as different as Nursing, Engineering, and Aesthetics and Cosmetics. The displayed passages do not show those words in context. They do, however, explicitly announce commentary on a shared written-response item in *Formação Geral* and comparison of common answers with an expected response. Together, these clues make shared item or response-analysis material a plausible source of the recurring vocabulary, without establishing whether particular words come from the prompt, the expected answer, or the commentary.

The excerpts themselves show a standardised reporting procedure: they distinguish objective from written-response items, explain item indices and scoring categories, and summarise performance by region and other groupings. This establishes the **reporting function** of the passages, but basic statistics alone do not explain the pole’s lexical profile. The full loading list also includes participation and administrative vocabulary such as *ingressante* (−.84), *comparecer* (−.49), and *convocação* (−.41), as well as technical terms such as *monotônico*, *decrescer*, and *gradiente*. The pole is therefore broader than the hazard scenario that stands out in the 2023 documents.

**Groups driving the pole.** The lowest mean is **2023_c6** (−8.67; *N* = 27), which supplies all ten examples, followed by **2009_c4** (−5.81; *N* = 27) and **2009_c6** (−2.93; *N* = 27). The c6 means for 2010 and 2011 are more modestly negative (−1.53 and −1.42), and no c5 group falls below −1. The concentration in 2023_c6 and 2009_c4 points to particular year–section combinations, not a stable feature of technical analysis or exam-perception sections across editions. The participation vocabulary offers a plausible lead for interpreting 2009_c4, but without excerpts from that group its role cannot be verified.

**Possible labels**
- **Documenting exam content and participation** — the better corpus-wide label: it accommodates both the item-related vocabulary illustrated by the 2023 documents and the participation strand in the full loadings.
- **Evaluating responses to applied exam tasks** — a more specific label for the dominant 2023_c6 manifestation: it captures how the reports relate student answers to an expected response, but is too narrow to explain the strong 2009_c4 mean on its own.
