## Factor f1 (negative): Exam appraisal through questions and responses

The negative pole captures discourse that **describes and appraises the exam through its questions, the responses they elicit, and the criteria used to interpret those responses**. In the technical-analysis excerpts, this function is most visible in the distinction between objective and open-ended items, the comparison of selected alternatives with the answer key, and the comparison of common responses with the expected answer. The group means show that the pole is also strongly associated with exam-perception reporting, so it should not be defined as technical item analysis alone.

**Corpus-wide lexical evidence.** Words such as *perguntar, detalhamento, ordem, esquema* and *compreensível* are consistent with describing what a question asks and how it is presented; *lembrar, apurar* and *julgar* suggest recall, inquiry and judgment, although their precise functions depend on context. The strongest loadings—*estar* (−1.28), *certo* (−.78) and *torno* (−.64)—are comparatively general and cannot establish the discourse function on their own. The pole also carries vocabulary from several substantive domains: *ciclista* and *acidentado* evoke road safety, *criminalização* and *humilhação* social or ethical issues, and *dióxido* and *carbono* environmental topics. This variety is consistent with reports taking up the subject matter of exam questions rather than with a single topic defining the pole.

**What the examples show.** All ten excerpts come from **2014_c6**, with scores from −11 to −9. Their largely shared opening presents regional performance statistics alongside a plan for item analysis: reporting difficulty and discrimination indices, relating chosen alternatives to performance, and assessing written answers against an expected response. Yet the full-document loading lists for all ten also contain *acidentado, ciclista, desvalorização, criminalização* and *banalização*. Their recurrence across courses as different as engineering, Letras and mathematics is consistent with shared General Education item or response material. The displayed openings do not establish where or how those words are used. The distinction matters: statistical reporting is prominent in the excerpts, but it does not account for much of the vocabulary that marks the pole.

**Groups driving the pole.** The strongest group is **2014_c6 (−8.53)**, followed by **2009_c4 (−7.33)**, **2010_c4 (−6.58)** and **2016_c6 (−5.22)**. Exam perception (c4) has negative means in every observed year and generally scores less strongly after 2010, though not monotonically. Technical analysis (c6) is more variable: 2014 is a pronounced peak, with stronger secondary years in 2016 and 2019 (−4.55). No concept-distribution group (c5) has a negative mean. Thus, 2014_c6 supplies the strongest concentration and all the illustrations, while c4 supplies the most consistent association across years. Without c4 excerpts, the exact form the discourse takes in the perception sections remains less directly evidenced.

**Possible labels**

- **Exam appraisal through questions and responses** — preferred because it covers both perception reporting and technical analysis while capturing the question- and response-related vocabulary.
- **Exam review and response evaluation** — emphasizes the evaluative function illustrated by answer keys, expected responses and correction criteria, without making either statistics or the recurrent 2014 topics the defining feature.
