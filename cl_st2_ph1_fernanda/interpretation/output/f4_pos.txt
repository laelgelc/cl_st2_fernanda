## f4 positive pole: **Content-sensitive exam analysis**

This pole is best interpreted as **technical reporting that relates student performance to what the exam asks and what counts as an adequate response**. The excerpts show the report’s evaluative procedures; the corpus-wide loadings show that the vocabulary distinguishing this pole is not primarily statistical. Much of it points instead to the substantive and linguistic material that enters the analysis of exam items and responses.

**Corpus-wide loadings.** The strongest variables include *públicas* (.93), *brasileira* (.91), *peso* (.90), and *centrado* (.85), but also *desordem*, *destruir*, and *fraternidade* (each .84), and *mentira* (.81). The wider set includes sociocultural and historical vocabulary (*étnico-racial*, *sócio-histórico-cultural*, *colonização*), terms relevant to language and interpretation (*personificar*, *tipologia*, *morfema*, *causalidade*), and more concrete or domain-specific words (*calça*, *advogado*, *cartório*, *divórcio*). This range is more consistent with reports bringing varied exam content into their analysis than with a single subject theme or a general register of statistical reporting. The loadings alone do not establish whether a given word comes from an item, an expected answer, a student response, or the report’s own commentary.

**What the examples add.** The ten displayed excerpts are all from 2018_c6, span several disciplines, and have scores of 13–16. Their openings present a common procedure: report score distributions, analyse objective items through difficulty and discrimination indices, and compare common constructed responses with expected answers, including commentary on Portuguese usage. The displayed passages mostly show the statistical opening, and *públicas* is directly visible in comparisons of public and private institutions. The full-document lists add a different kind of evidence: all ten contain *destruir*, *mentira*, *personificar*, *étnico-racial*, and *sócio-histórico-cultural*. Alongside that shared vocabulary, there are differences such as *calça* and *aceleração* in Fashion Design, *advogado* and *assento* in Law, and *colonização* in Social Work. This combination is compatible with a shared exam component alongside material that varies by course, though the lists do not locate the words in particular items or report subsections.

**Groups driving the pole.** The leading means are **2018_c6 (13.05), 2015_c6 (12.08), 2017_c6 (9.56), and 2013_c6 (8.06)**. Their relatively low within-group standard deviations (0.43–1.23) indicate that these peaks are not driven by a few exceptional reports. Other c6 groups show more modest positive means, including 2016 (3.83), 2021 (3.00), and 2014 (2.53), while no c4 or c5 mean reaches 2. The pole is therefore strongly associated with technical exam analysis in particular editions, not with c6 uniformly or with a steady increase over time. The 2018 examples illustrate the strongest group; they do not establish that the other peak years feature the same topics.

**Possible labels**
- **Content-sensitive exam analysis** — preferred because it captures the integration of technical evaluation and substantive exam content without restricting the pole to a single item type.
- **Item and response evaluation** — a more function-specific label that foregrounds the procedures described in the excerpts, especially analysis of item performance and comparison of student responses with expected answers.
