## f2 (positive): Statistical reporting of students’ exam perceptions

The positive pole captures how ENADE reports turn students’ judgments about the exam into **comparable survey evidence**. The reports explain what the questionnaire measures, how students and responses are grouped, and how readers should interpret percentages and differences between groups. The defining function is not simply to report what students thought, but to make the basis for comparing their responses explicit.

**Corpus-wide loadings.** *Questionário* (1.18), *indagar* (.95), and *estudantes* (.70) anchor the survey component. The co-loading of *ordenar* (1.13), *subconjunto* (1.11), and *recorte* (1.09) points to the construction of groups for comparison, while *percentagem* (1.11), *arredondamento* (1.00), and *totalizar* (.80) mark the conventions used to summarize and present responses. *Estimador* (1.28), *disjunto* (1.21), *interseção* (.87), and *parâmetro* (.56) add a further function: explaining how the report decides which differences to highlight. The broader educational vocabulary, including *coordenador* (1.01), *avaliativo* (.76), and *pedagógico* (.55), situates this statistical reporting within an institutional assessment context without making pedagogy the dominant theme.

**High-scoring texts.** All ten examples score 24 and come from `2023_c4`. Across course areas, they repeat a common sequence: introduce the nine-item questionnaire, distinguish percentile cut points from the four subsets of scores they define, and explain how response percentages and confidence intervals are displayed. Several excerpts then apply that framework to regional comparisons of perceived difficulty. The Farmácia excerpt, for instance, combines some response options into “Difícil” or “Muito difícil” and identifies which regional differences the report treats as statistically significant. All ten full documents have the same 24 reported loading words. That shared profile includes both terms whose function is visible in the passages, such as *subconjunto* and *interseção*, and terms such as *coordenador* and *contrapartida* whose role is not established by the displayed text. The repetition across course areas suggests a shared 2023 reporting template, not ten independent demonstrations of how the pole is realized across years.

**Groups driving the pole.** The main drivers are the later `c4` exam-perception groups: `2023_c4` (23.30), `2022_c4` (20.77), `2021_c4` (18.57), `2019_c4` (16.21), and `2018_c4` (15.55). The `c4` mean rises from 0.22 in 2009 to 8.55 in 2011, stays roughly between 10.5 and 11.9 in 2012–2017, and then rises again from 2018 onward. No `c5` group has a positive mean, and `c6` makes a smaller contribution, most notably in 2022 (5.96). This pattern locates the pole primarily in exam-perception reporting and shows that its lexical profile becomes more pronounced in later editions. The shared wording in the 2023 examples suggests that report design may contribute to that rise, but the excerpts alone cannot establish what changed in earlier editions.

**Possible labels**

- **Statistical reporting of students’ exam perceptions** — preferred because it names both the subject of the reports and what the discourse does with it.
- **Making exam-survey responses comparable** — emphasizes the work of defining groups, aggregating answers, and explaining how differences are interpreted.
