## Factor f2 (negative pole): **Reporting and interpreting rating distributions**

This pole captures a discourse that **reports how ENADE course ratings (*conceitos*) are distributed and guides readers in interpreting those distributions**. It combines two functions: summarising where ratings concentrate across regions and institutional categories, and explaining how tables and graphs represent concentrations, extremes, and exceptions.

**Corpus-wide lexical evidence.** *Distribuir* (−.58), *predominar* (−.47), and *alocar* (−.37) support the reporting of how cases fall into categories and which categories are most common. *Coincidente* (−.51), *negrito* (−.50), *identificador* (−.49), and *atípico* (−.30) point to a more specific function: explaining how to read statistical displays. The excerpts clarify the role of the otherwise general *dito* (−.56): in *a caixa propriamente dita*, it helps distinguish the box itself from other elements of the plot. Other loadings, such as *grandeza*, *bimodal*, and *elevado*, are compatible with broader statistical description. Still others, such as *creche*, *câmera*, and *homicídio*, do not fit the rating-distribution interpretation directly. The label therefore names the clearest shared discourse function, not a single topic that accounts for all 36 loadings.

**What the examples show.** The ten excerpts use a recurring sequence across different courses: introduce a table of counts and percentages, explain the corresponding box plot, and then identify predominant ratings and exceptions nationally and region by region. They do not simply list results: they explain why a plot can collapse to a median bar, why marks may coincide, and how outliers are flagged or identified. All ten full documents share eight reported loading words—*distribuir, dito, coincidente, negrito, identificador, predominar, habilitado,* and *atípico*—and the Marketing report additionally has *grandeza*. This document-level evidence matters where the excerpts cut off before the wording about regional identifiers or course eligibility. The repeated phrasing across subject areas is consistent with a shared reporting template.

**Which groups drive the pole.** **2009_c5** is the clear driver (mean **−7.85**, *N* = 27, SD = 0.46), and all ten examples come from it, with scores of −8 or −9. The next-lowest group, **2011_c6**, is substantially weaker (−3.79). Every later c5 group still has a negative mean (−2.22 to −0.50), with 2016_c5 and 2017_c5 at the more negative end. The c6 scores are less consistent across years: 2013_c6 also shows some affinity (−2.18), but no c4 group has a negative mean. Thus, the pole has a recurring association with reporting rating distributions, while its strongest expression is specific to the 2009 c5 reporting format. The c6 means suggest that the pole is not exclusive to c5, but the supplied excerpts do not establish whether c6 realises it through the same box-plot explanations.

**Alternative labels**
- **Comparative reporting of course ratings** — foregrounds national, regional, and institutional comparisons, but gives less prominence to guidance on reading the displays.
- **Guided reading of statistical distributions** — foregrounds the explanation of graphical conventions and unusual values, while leaving room for the pole’s weaker presence in technical-analysis sections.
