**Preferred label: Reporting exam performance and item content.** The negative pole of f3 marks an edition-specific form of technical exam reporting. The excerpts show a standardised account of cohort performance, while the corpus-wide loadings point to social, ethical, and expressive language that may come from exam questions, source texts, or item commentary elsewhere in the same documents. The pole is therefore more specific than “statistical reporting,” but the evidence does not establish exactly where the thematic vocabulary occurs.

**What the loadings indicate.** The strongest loadings are not predominantly statistical. Words such as *esperança, corresponsável, entrave, autoestima,* and *humilhação* evoke aspirations, responsibility, obstacles, and personal experience. Others, such as *maioridade, divórcio, homicídio,* and *inadimplência*, evoke legal and social issues, while *eloquente, elegância, exprimir,* and *originalidade* point to expression and its evaluation. This mix is consistent with reports that reproduce or discuss varied exam material, rather than with a single topic or a uniformly evaluative stance on the part of the report writers. *Ingressante* (−.59) is a clear link to the cohort-reporting function visible in the excerpts.

**What the examples show.** All ten excerpts come from 2010_c6 and score −7, except Occupational Therapy at −8. Across courses, they follow nearly the same template: define the statistics and score intervals, report attendance and score distributions, and use means, medians, and graphs to substantiate comparisons between *Ingressantes* and *Concluintes*. Yet all ten full documents share the same seven reported loading words—*esperança, corresponsável, entrave, eloquente, baila, codificar,* and *horizonte*—and Occupational Therapy adds *alternância*. These words are not visible in the displayed passages. Their recurrence across courses suggests shared edition-level material in the full reports, not ten independent examples of thematic language in the statistical prose. The excerpts establish the reporting context; the document-level words help explain what distinguishes these texts lexically.

**Which groups drive the pole.** The lowest means belong to **2010_c6 (−6.89)** and **2012_c6 (−6.82)**. Their small within-group SDs (0.46 and 0.39) show that the pattern is consistent across reports in those groups. **2016_c6 (−4.78)** also contributes substantially, with weaker contributions from **2009_c6 (−2.89)** and **2013_c6 (−1.65)**. These are the only groups with negative means; no c4 or c5 group has a negative mean. The pattern is thus concentrated in particular editions of the technical-analysis section, not characteristic of c6 in every year. Because the examples are all from 2010, they do not establish that 2012 or 2016 uses the same wording or template.

A more conservative label is **Technical reporting with exam-content vocabulary**. It captures the co-occurrence of the two kinds of discourse without implying that the thematic words have been located in item commentary. **Cohort performance reporting** describes the displayed passages well, but on its own leaves most of the strongest loadings unexplained.
